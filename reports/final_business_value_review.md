# Final Business Value Review

Verification date: `2026-04-23`

## What is strong

- The final segments are understandable without reading the code first.
- Segment names are concrete and business-usable: `VIP Spenders`, `Subscription Loyalists`, `Engaged Free Players`, `Steady Core Players`, and `Lapsing Casuals`.
- The main business takeaway is clear and credible: revenue is highly concentrated in a small payer cohort.
- Recommendations are tied to observed behavior instead of generic gaming advice.
- The core visuals support decisions: the size/revenue chart shows concentration, the heatmap shows behavioral differences, and the impact/ease matrix helps prioritize action.

## What was vague or weak

- The previous summary was slightly too analysis-shaped for a senior business stakeholder and could be tightened.
- Top-level report clutter made the final submission story less clean than it should be.
- Notebook execution evidence was present in older reports, but not yet captured in the exact final report format requested.

## What was fixed

- Tightened `deliverables/summary.md` into a clearer executive narrative with a compact segment table.
- Archived superseded checkpoint reports so the top-level `reports/` folder reads as a final submission set.
- Added final review documents that separate assignment coverage, execution evidence, business value, GitHub readiness, and verdict.

## What remains acceptable but imperfect

- The segmentation is portfolio-level because the behavioral tables do not include `game_id`.
- The recommendations are intentionally concise and do not go as far as experiment design, target rules, or estimated revenue lift.
- Two low-value segments contribute effectively zero revenue, so monetization differentiation there is driven more by engagement and recency than by spend behavior.
